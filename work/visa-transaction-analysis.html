The question

Growing the value of existing cardholders is cheaper than acquiring new ones. Which customers drive transaction value, what makes them spend more, and where does fraud concentrate?

The data

Card transactions from January 2019 to July 2020: customer demographics, merchant and category, amount, timestamp, cardholder and merchant location, and a fraud flag.

Approach

  1. 01

    Clean

    Dropped index artefacts, parsed dates, derived customer age, filled missing job and ZIP fields, and documented every column.

  2. 02

    Engineer

    Added hour, weekday and month features, plus the distance between cardholder and merchant — a classic fraud signal.

  3. 03

    Explore

    EDA on spend by category, weekday, hour, gender and month, with a correlation heatmap.

  4. 04

    Test

    Ran ten hypothesis tests — e.g. do frequent users spend more, do weekend shoppers have higher CLV, is fraud more likely between midnight and 6 AM, does merchant diversity raise CLV.

  5. 05

    Segment

    Built RFM (recency, frequency, monetary) segments to separate Champions from at-risk and dormant customers, then visualised it all in Tableau.

What I found

  • Most transactions happen between 12 PM and 3 PM; Sunday and Monday are the busiest days.
  • By frequency, gas/transport, grocery and in-store shopping lead; by spend, grocery, online shopping and entertainment lead.
  • Fraud clusters in the odd hours (12 AM – 6 AM) and in online shopping and grocery — and it spans low and high amounts, not just big tickets.
  • RFM segments give marketing a ready target list: reward Champions, re-engage dormant high-value customers.

What I'd recommend

  • Add time-of-day rules to fraud monitoring for the midnight–6 AM window.
  • Apply category-level checks to online shopping and grocery transactions.
  • Run weekend and multi-category campaigns aimed at the highest-CLV segments.
