-- Top 3 pizzas by revenue in each category
SELECT name, revenue
FROM (
  SELECT category, name, revenue,
         RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY category
                      ORDER BY revenue DESC) AS rn
  FROM (
    SELECT pt.category, pt.name,
           SUM(od.quantity * p.price) AS revenue
    FROM pizza_types pt
    JOIN pizzas p ON pt.pizza_type_id = p.pizza_type_id
    JOIN order_details od ON od.pizza_id = p.pizza_id
    GROUP BY pt.category, pt.name
  ) AS by_pizza
) AS ranked
WHERE rn <= 3;

The question

A pizza shop wants to know what sells, when, and what earns the most — so it can plan its menu, staffing and inventory.

The data

Four related CSVs: orders (timestamps), order details (pizza and quantity), pizzas (size and price) and pizza types (name, category, ingredients).

Approach

  1. 01

    Basic

    Total orders and revenue, highest-priced pizza, most common size, top 5 pizzas by quantity.

  2. 02

    Intermediate

    Quantity by category, orders by hour, daily average pizzas, top 3 by revenue.

  3. 03

    Advanced

    Revenue share by category, cumulative revenue over time, and the top 3 pizzas per category with RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY …).

What I found

  • A full year (2015) of trading: 21,350 orders, 49,574 pizzas and $817.9K revenue — about 138 pizzas a day.
  • Orders peak at lunch, 12–1 PM, with a second rush from 5–7 PM — the two staffing windows that matter.
  • Large is the most-ordered size; XL and XXL together are only about 1% of order lines.
  • The Classic Deluxe sells the most units (2,453), but the Thai Chicken earns the most revenue ($43.4K) — volume and value aren't the same list.
  • Revenue is spread evenly: Classic leads at 26.9%, and the other three categories sit between 23.7% and 25.5%.
  • The Brie Carre is the weakest earner at $11.6K — a candidate for a menu review.

What I'd recommend

  • Staff and prep for the lunch peak first, then the early-evening rush.
  • Promote high-value chicken pizzas alongside the best-selling Classic Deluxe.
  • Review or reposition the lowest-revenue pizzas, and consider dropping XXL.
